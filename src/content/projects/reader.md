# Case study, shown on this project's own page at /work/<slug>/.
problem: >-
  My books arrive in four shapes. A publisher's EPUB, a PDF someone scanned
  out of a 1940s hardback, a Word manuscript a friend sent me, a text file
  off a mailing list. Four apps, four ideas of where I stopped reading, four
  places my highlights lived. The scanned one was not text at all, only
  pictures of pages, so I could not search it, highlight a line or make the
  type bigger. Two of those apps wanted an account before they would open a
  file that was already sitting on my phone.

approach: >-
  A mark belongs to the words it covers, not to the spot on the screen where
  it happened to land. So the app remembers the words you marked and nothing
  about how the page looked when you marked them. Turn the type up, widen
  the margins, switch to dark, rotate the tablet, and every highlight is
  still on the same sentences and you are still on the same words. The place
  you stopped works the same way, which is why changing the type size does
  not lose your page. The other decision was to make going online impossible
  rather than optional. Nothing in the app can open a connection, and I
  check the finished app for one instead of trusting myself to remember.

next: >-
  It is not in any store. I put it on my own phone myself and read on it
  every day. Two things I know are wrong. Pictures inside a PDF do not
  survive the text being re-wrapped: a figure ends up at the top of the page
  it came from rather than where it sat, and plenty do not come through at
  all, so reading that book as its original pages is the answer for now. The
  other is following along with an audiobook you supplied yourself. The app
  knows where each track begins and guesses between those points, so it
  drifts by a sentence or two. Narration it speaks itself is exact, because
  it knows which sentence it is saying. Closing that gap is what I am on
  next.

capabilities:
  - title: "One shelf for four kinds of file"
    detail: >-
      EPUB, PDF, Word documents and text files all land in the same library
      and read the same way afterwards. What a file is gets worked out by
      looking inside it, so a wrong or missing extension does not matter,
      and importing the same book twice does not give you two of it.
  - title: "Read aloud with the page following"
    detail: >-
      Pick a voice and it reads to you. The sentence being spoken is tinted,
      a marker slides along under the words, and the page turns only once
      the words have left it. Turn a page by hand and it stops chasing you;
      one button puts you back with the voice.
  - title: "Highlights that survive a change of type"
    detail: >-
      Marks are held against the words, so changing typeface, size, line
      spacing, margins, colour scheme or the way you are holding the device
      never moves them.
  - title: "Scanned pages become text you can use"
    detail: >-
      Read the pages of a scan and the book is rebuilt as text, after which
      it re-wraps, searches, highlights and can be read aloud like anything
      else. The original pages are kept, because reading words off a picture
      is a lossy business and you may want to check it.
  - title: "Two ways to read a PDF"
    detail: >-
      Text that re-wraps for prose, or the original fixed pages for tables,
      forms and anything whose meaning is in its arrangement. The choice is
      per book and changes only what you look at: chapters, notes and search
      all survive the switch.
  - title: "Notes that outlive the app"
    detail: >-
      Every book you have annotated is written out as its own text file,
      quotes and notes in the order they appear in the book, somewhere you
      can reach from outside the app. Everything else goes when the app
      goes.

Reader takes ebooks, PDFs, documents and plain text and turns them into one
library where every book opens the same way, with the typeface, size, spacing,
margins and page colour you set. Attach a recording you own, or have the app read
the book aloud in a voice you choose. Change the type size and both your
highlights and your place stay on the same words.
